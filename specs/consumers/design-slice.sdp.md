# A slice reads a Pack as one design, with the architecture it touches and what each member needs next

Features that must be reviewed together are already grouped as a Pack, and the Pack page shows each member in turn. A design pass also needs the whole at once: how the members rest on each other, which components they touch, realized or planned, which decisions and open questions bound them, and what each member needs for its next rung. Rounds of design add detail a little at a time; the floor sets how little a rung needs, and how much is enough stays a judgment the authoring guidance supports and no check makes.

The first generation kept a slice as an authored file: a list of members and the one question they answer together, a saved view for design and review that delivered nothing itself (`@libar-dev/architect`, `formal-spec/04-tag-registry.md`, the hierarchy level `slice`). The original design saved AI context slices per Pack, per capability and per changed Spec as files (`docs/lineage/v0-design/07-spec-studio-and-projections.md` §5.5). The Protocol holds the first as a Pack, its members in reading order and its framing saying what they answer together, and it refused the second: a context bundle is composed on each invocation and never saved. A design slice is therefore a reading and not an artifact. It is a Pack read whole, narrowed to one perspective's members when a pass reads for one concern, or an ordered list of Specs, composed from the graph each time, and it adds no grouping, kind, stage or gate. A Spec's planning slice reads one Spec's neighborhood; widening it hop by hop would pick a reach no one authored, so a design slice starts from the set a person chose.

The reading answers what a card-by-card page leaves a reviewer to assemble. A design that builds on existing code says so through its own `dependsOn` or `refines` to the Spec that code satisfies, so the architecture a design ahead of its code will touch is read from those relations and the anchors of the Specs they name. The first adopter asked for the same separation: the design under review apart from the contracts and decisions it rests on, and the outside Spec whose rung holds a member shown at the boundary with what it holds (`libar-platform/docs/feedback/sdp-feedback-02.md`, items 11 and 12).

## Intent

- actor: A person or an agent running a design pass over features that must be reviewed together.
- problem: The Pack page reads member by member, so a reviewer assembles the dependency order, the touched architecture and the open decisions by hand before judging the design as a whole.
- outcome: Read a Pack as one design slice: its members and their relations, the components they touch, realized and planned, the decisions and open questions that bound them, and what each member needs for its next rung.
- value: A design pass judges the whole before its parts, starting from the members others rest on, and sees how much design each member carries beside its rung without a score.

## Behavior

- rule: [#readingNotArtifact] A design slice is a reading of a set of Specs that a catalog recipe composes from the graph on each invocation; it is never authored, saved or committed, it adds no grouping, namespace, kind, stage or gate, and reading it records nothing.
- rule: [#scope] A slice takes one scope: a Pack, its resolved members read in authored order, or an ordered list of Spec ids; a Pack scope may be narrowed to an ordered subset of its members, such as the members the perspective filter selects, and then says that it is a subset.
- rule: [#restsOn] Inside the slice a member rests on each other member it names by `refines`, `dependsOn`, `constrainedBy` or `decidedBy`, except a `refines` to a member that names it back by one of the other three, as the statement queue reads them; every relation of those four types between two members is listed with its type, its reason and whether it orders, so a reading tells an ordering link from a relation that orders nothing.
- rule: [#depth] A member that rests on no other member has depth 1, as the statement queue's rounds start at 1, and any other member has the depth after the deepest member it rests on; members that reach each other through those links form a cycle, share the depth after the deepest member outside the cycle that any of them rests on, and are listed with one closed path, and the slice refuses nothing.
- rule: [#examplesFold] An example member folds into the member it refines when that `refines` is its only relation of the four types, in either direction, inside the slice or across its boundary: it is listed among that member's example members and keeps its own row, with its own stated rung, floor reached, stated next rung and holds, and folding lends the member none of them; an example with any other such relation is a member like any other.
- rule: [#memberColumns] Each member carries its stated rung, the floor reached, its stated next rung with the clauses that hold it, its design columns, its rule count as rule coverage counts it, its verifiers and verifying examples with how many are enabled, the units that implement and reference it, and the components those units belong to; the columns are counts and lists beside the rung, never a score, a verdict or a rung.
- rule: [#boundary] The slice's boundary is built over its members as Pack design builds a Pack's, so a whole Pack's slice has the Pack's boundary; each Spec the slice rests on also carries the floor it reaches and the members whose stated next rung it holds, and a Pack member left out of a narrowed slice is marked as in the Pack.
- rule: [#touchedComponents] The components a slice touches are the anchored components it reaches three ways, each named on the component's row and never merged: a unit that satisfies or references a member belongs to it; a unit that satisfies a Spec the slice rests on belongs to it; or a member component design names it.
- rule: [#componentDesigns] Every member that is a component design, as the planned-architecture convention selects it, is its own row whether or not code realizes it: the component it names, the layer, context, role and uses it declares, whether an anchor holds that component, whether that anchor satisfies the design, and the realization comparison's differing and unreadable rows for it; a declared value never stands in for an anchored one, nor an anchored value for a declared one.
- rule: [#touchedUses] Between touched components the slice lists the anchored `uses` aggregated to component pairs, as the architecture map counts them, a use within one component left out, each pair with the unit edges behind its count; each component a member design declares in `uses` is a planned use of that design, listed apart from the anchored pairs and never added to their counts.
- rule: [#outsideNeighbors] A component one `uses` step from a touched component, in either direction, or named in a member design's `uses`, that the slice does not touch is listed as outside the slice with how it is reached, and its anchored pairs with touched components are listed and marked outside; the slice reaches no further, and outside components and pairs are counted apart from the touched ones.
- rule: [#unplaced] A unit that implements or references a member and belongs to no component is listed apart, so no binding drops out of the reading.
- rule: [#notDownstream] The slice does not count the code of Specs that rest on it: that code follows a change to the slice, and design-change impact reads it; a component that uses a touched component appears only as an outside neighbor.
- rule: [#decisionsInScope] The decisions in scope are the decision members and every decision a member names through `decidedBy`, each with its stated rung, the floor reached, whether it is a member, the members it shapes, the decisions it refines and supersedes, and its open and blocking question counts.
- rule: [#questionsInScope] The open questions in scope are those of every member and of every decision in scope, and the blocking questions of every Spec outside the slice that holds a member's stated next rung, blocking questions first; each carries its address, its fields and the members whose stated next rung its Spec holds.
- rule: [#noDesignVerdict] The slice shows how much design each member carries and never whether it is enough: it reports no over- or under-designed member, no ratio of design to rung and no finding, because how much to write is the authoring guidance's judgment and checks police conformance and honesty, never content quality.
- rule: [#graphOnly] The slice reads declared relations, anchored bindings and component designs only; it infers no dependency, component or decision from prose, paths, imports or names.
- rule: [#orderAdvisory] Depth gives a reading order, the members others rest on first; it records, schedules and gates nothing, and a member read or designed out of that order is never a finding.

## Design

- sliceRecipe: recipe 54, the design slice, takes exactly one scope, `params.pack` or `params.specs`, an optional `params.members` beside `pack`, and an optional `params.detail`; an unknown parameter name returns `{ unknownParams, accepts }` alone, and any input `#design.sliceDiagnostics` refuses returns `{ diagnostics }` and no slice.
- sliceDiagnostics: the checks run in this order and every failure is kept, a check that reads an earlier value being skipped when that value failed: a `detail` that is present and neither a boolean nor a list of strings, null included, gives `{ scope: "detail", problem: "Expected true, false or a list of member ids." }`; neither or both of `pack` and `specs` gives `{ scope: "scope", problem: "Supply exactly one of pack or specs." }`; a `pack` that is not a string naming a Pack gives `{ scope: "pack", problem: "Pack does not resolve." }`; beside a resolved Pack, a `members` that is not a non-empty list of distinct strings gives `{ scope: "members", problem: "Expected a non-empty list of distinct member ids." }`, and otherwise each id that is no resolved member gives `{ scope: <the id>, problem: "Not a resolved member of the Pack." }` in list order; a `specs` that is not a non-empty list of distinct strings gives `{ scope: "specs", problem: "Expected a non-empty list of distinct Spec ids." }`, and otherwise each id the graph does not hold gives `{ scope: <the id>, problem: "Spec does not resolve." }` in list order, as the context bundle names an unresolved id; and a `members` beside `specs` gives `{ scope: "members", problem: "members narrows only a Pack scope." }`.
- sliceDetail: `params.detail` is absent or `false` for the summary, `true` for every row in full, or a list of member ids for full rows of those members beside summary rows of the rest, an id that is no member adding `{ id, reason: "not-member" }` to the slice's `diagnostics`, as Pack design reads it; a summary keeps `totals` whole and adds `leftOut`, one line for each kind of detail it omits.
- sliceAnswer: `{ scope, totals, members, structure, boundary, architecture, decisions, questions }`, where `scope` is `{ pack, title, framing, subset, members, unresolved }` for a Pack, `unresolved` naming the manifest ids the graph does not hold, and `{ specs }` for a Spec list.
- sliceTotals: `{ members, exampleMembers, relations, depth, cycles, heldBelowNextRung, waitingForAuthor, restsOn, holdingOutside, restedOnBy, verifiedBy, supersededBy, components: { touched, throughMembers, throughRestsOn, throughDesigns, outside }, designs: { total, realized, matching, unrealized, differing, unreadable }, componentUses: { inside, outside, planned }, decisions, decisionsBelowReady, openQuestions, blockingQuestions }`, where `exampleMembers` counts folded example members, `depth` is the deepest member's depth, `holdingOutside` counts the outside Specs that hold a member, `heldBelowNextRung` and `waitingForAuthor` count as Pack design counts them, `components.touched` counts distinct anchored components however reached and `components.outside` the outside components, `designs` counts member designs as the realization comparison's totals count designs, and `componentUses` counts the anchored pairs inside, the anchored pairs marked outside, and the planned uses.
- memberRow: `{ id, title, specKind, altitude, statedReadiness, floorReached, statedNextRung, holds, depth, restsOn, restedOnBy, exampleOf, exampleMembers, design, rules: { total, keyed }, examples: { total, enabled }, verifiers: { total, enabled }, implementedBy, referencedBy, components }` in the scope's order, where `holds` lists the stated-next-rung failures as `[{ clauseId, description, targets: [{ type, id, statedReadiness }] }]`, `restsOn` and `restedOnBy` are member ids, `exampleOf` is the member an example member refines or null, `exampleMembers` the folded example members, `design` is the design columns as `{ entries, declarations, openQuestions, blockingQuestions, decisions }` counts, `examples` the verifying examples bound to the member wherever they sit, apart from `exampleMembers`, `rules.total` is the keyed and unkeyed rule and flow entries of the primary behavior owner, `implementedBy` and `referencedBy` are unit ids, and `components` the ids of the components those units belong to.
- memberSummary: `{ id, specKind, statedReadiness, floorReached, statedNextRung, holds, depth, restsOn, exampleOf, design, rules, implementedBy, referencedBy }` is a member's summary row, with `holds` as `[{ clauseId, targets: [id] }]`, as Pack design's summary reads it, and `implementedBy` and `referencedBy` as counts.
- structureShape: `{ edges: [{ from, to, type, orders, reason }], cycles: [{ members, cycle }] }`, one edge per declared typed relation between two members, sorted by `from`, `type` and `to`, `orders` false only for a `refines` the target names back, and `reason` present only when the relation states one; `cycles` in the statement queue's cycle row; a summary drops each edge's `reason`.
- boundaryRow: Pack design's boundary row `{ id, title, statedReadiness, implemented, via }` with `specKind`, `floorReached`, `holds`, the member ids whose stated-next-rung failures name it as a target, empty outside `restsOn`, and `inPack: true` on a Pack member left out of a narrowed slice, in `{ restsOn, restedOnBy, verifiedBy, supersededBy }` sorted by id; a summary keeps `{ id, statedReadiness, holds }` and `inPack`.
- componentRow: `{ id, label, layer, context, members: { satisfies, references }, restsOn, designs }` sorted by id, one per touched anchored component, where `members.satisfies` and `members.references` are the members whose units belong to it by each binding, `restsOn` the outside Specs the slice rests on whose satisfying units belong to it, `designs` the member component designs naming it, and `label`, `layer` and `context` come from the architecture map's component row.
- componentSummary: `{ id, layer, context, members: { satisfies, references }, restsOn, designs }`, each list replaced by its count and `label` left out.
- designRow: `{ spec, componentId, layer, context, role, uses, anchorExists, realized, differing, unreadable }` sorted by `spec`, one per member component design, where the declared values are read as spec:model.planned-architecture#design.designKeys reads them, null when unstated or unreadable and `uses` an empty list for `none`, `realized` holds as spec:model.planned-architecture#design.realizedWhen says, `differing` lists the comparison's `{ field, planned, realized }` rows for the design and `unreadable` its `{ key, value }` rows; a summary keeps `{ spec, componentId, anchorExists, realized }` and the two lists as counts.
- componentUse: `{ from, to, count, outside, units }` sorted by `from` and `to`, one per pair of components with an anchored `uses` between them and at least one end touched, where `count` is the architecture map's count of unit `uses` edges for the pair, `units` lists those edges as `{ from, to, claim }` from the architecture map's `usesEdges`, and `outside` is true when one end is not touched; a summary drops `units`.
- plannedUse: `{ design, from, to, outside }` sorted by `from`, `to` and `design`, one per component a member design declares in `uses` other than its own, where `from` is the component the design names and `outside` is true when `to` is neither touched nor named by a member design.
- outsideRow: `{ id, label, layer, context, anchored, reachedBy }` sorted by id, where `reachedBy` lists, in this order, `touchedUses` when a touched component uses it, `usesTouched` when it uses a touched component, and `plannedUse` when a member design declares it, and `anchored` says whether an anchor holds it; a summary keeps `{ id, anchored, reachedBy }`.
- architectureShape: `{ components, designs, uses, plannedUses, outside, unplaced }`, where `unplaced` lists `{ unit, member, via }`, `via` being `satisfies` or `references`, for each unit of a member that belongs to no component.
- decisionRow: `{ id, title, statedReadiness, floorReached, member, shapes, refines, supersedes, supersededBy, openQuestions, blockingQuestions }` sorted by id, where `shapes` lists the members that name the decision through `decidedBy` and the next three lists come from its decision register row; a summary keeps `{ id, statedReadiness, member, shapes }` with `shapes` as a count.
- questionRow: `{ address, spec, origin, index, question, blocking, deferral, decider, links, provisional, trigger, holds }` with the open-question register's fields, where `origin` is `member`, `decision` or `restsOn`, `index` the question's authored position from 0, `address` null without a key, and `holds` the members whose stated next rung its Spec holds when the question blocks, its own Spec first when that Spec is a member held by the floor's blocking-question clause, empty for a non-blocking question; rows sort blocking first, then by how many members they hold, then by Spec id and position; a summary keeps a non-blocking question as `{ address, spec, index }`.
