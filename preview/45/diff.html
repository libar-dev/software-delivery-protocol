rule.decisionAdmission · added

Now

The skill teaches admitting a decision, as a Decision section or a decision Spec, only for a choice that stays true after the work it shapes ships, is hard to reverse, would surprise a reader without its context and was made against a real alternative. It names what never qualifies, with the home each takes: a plan or sequence of work, a status or deliverables list, today's realization, and a why the existing design already forced. Its first rationale says why the choice is a decision at all. An admitted choice sits where the section and kind duality puts it: inline in the one Spec it shapes by default, and a decision Spec of its own when another Spec rests on it or it needs its own identity, readiness or review; how many Specs it shapes never decides whether it is a decision. No check reads this guidance.

rule.entryWriting · added

Now

The skill teaches writing each entry so a reader can check it: an outcome names the change for its actor without the mechanism, a rule states one obligation, an example binds literal values, a question names one choice with its decider and the asker's lean, and why is said once. rule.buildFromSpec · added

Now

The skill teaches building a Spec from its graph context: read the law it rests on, what it guarantees and who verifies it, the open questions on it and on each Spec it rests on, its pinned declarations, and its keyed rules no enabled example exercises; write the verifying tests before the code; hold the code to the pinned declarations and rules, leaving private names, decomposition and the algorithm to the build. The build proceeds on a non-blocking question's provisional reading and leaves the question open; a blocking question goes to its decider, and the code that answers it waits; a choice the Spec leaves open without a question returns to the Spec as a question or a change proposal, never settled in code. Close reads the Spec's delivery facts and the drift alarm, which lists a Spec built below `ready` until its owner states the rung, so the handoff names each listed Spec and states no rung for the owner. Building below `ready` stays ordinary work.
